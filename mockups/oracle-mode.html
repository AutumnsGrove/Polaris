Polaris · design exploration

Oracle mode

Mockups for docs/plans/oracle-mode.md. Jev reads the prompt before the turn starts, picks a focus mode and adds a few nudges to the system prompt. These screens explore how that shows up: the moment it happens, the chip on the reply, the "why" sheet, smart chips, and the composer. Tap the Replay buttons to rerun an animation.

A · The moment

Reading the stars

What plays in the short gap between sending and the first token. Jev answers every check in one request, so the choreography is a fixed ~1.5s sequence that ends early if the answer starts streaming. It isn't one light per real answer.

A1

Constellation

Is it safe to take ibuprofen with lisinopril?

Usually best avoided for regular use. NSAIDs like ibuprofen can blunt the blood-pressure-lowering effect of ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril 1 and, together, raise the risk of kidney injury 2…

One faint star per check. They brighten in turn, a thin line joins them, and the constellation folds down into the chip. The most literal take on "reading the stars", and the most motion.

Single star

The Oracle star breathes twice, then settles and the result slides out beside it. The quietest option and the closest to "calm over clever". The star stays in place as the chip's icon.

A3

Sky dial

Six points around the star light clockwise, one per check, like a sextant sighting. More structured than A1 and more legible as "it checked six things", but it reads slightly more like a loading spinner.

B · The result

How the decision sits on the reply

Each variant shows two states: something fired, and nothing fired. The plan leaves open whether a quiet turn shows a bare glyph or nothing at all.

B1

Pill chip

Nudges fired
Nothing fired

Filled, tappable, sits above the answer. Clear and obviously interactive. When nothing fires, a dim bare star confirms Oracle ran.

B2

Margin note

Nudges fired
Nothing fired

No container, just a line of dim text like an editor's note. Most "reading-first". A quiet turn shows nothing. Easier to overlook that it's tappable.

B3

Corner star

Nudges fired

Only a star in the reply's corner; everything else lives in the sheet. The calmest option, but you can't see what Oracle decided without tapping, which weakens "sourcing is the product".

The why sheet

Opens from the chip. Shows every check's pick and Jev's probability, the nudge text the model actually received, and one-tap reruns with a different choice.

D · Offers

Smart chips

These come from the same Jev call, so they're ready before the LLM-generated follow-ups. They're offers only, and dismissing one hides it for the rest of the thread.

D1

Dashed pills above follow-ups

What's the current price of a Steam Deck OLED?
How does it compare to the LCD model?
Is a refurbished one worth it?

A dashed outline marks these as offers, not actions or answers. The pills wrap on narrow screens.

D2

Quiet lines

Any news on the Artemis III launch date?
What caused the last delay?
Who's on the crew?

Full-width lines with the verb on the right. More room to say what will happen. It sits more calmly next to the follow-up list, but takes more vertical space.

E · Controls

Composer and settings

When Oracle is on, the composer's "More" trigger says so. Picking a mode by hand for one message overrides Oracle for that message only.

Smart suggestions get their own switch, so you can keep Oracle's steering without the offers or the other way round. Both rely on the same single Jev call.

F · Changes

When Oracle changes course mid-thread

A focus-mode change partway through a thread needs higher confidence than the first pick, and it's never silent.

Your call

What to pick

  1. The moment: A1 constellation, A2 single star, or A3 sky dial? My pick is A2. It's the calmest, and the star it leaves behind becomes the chip's icon, so nothing disappears.
  2. The result: B1 pill, B2 margin note, or B3 corner star? My pick is B1, with the dim bare star on quiet turns so you can always tell Oracle ran.
  3. Why sheet: C1 odds or C2 sentences? C1 is better for tuning; C2 is friendlier. It could be C2 with C1's rows behind a "Details" toggle.
  4. Offers: D1 pills or D2 lines?
  5. The glyph itself: does a long-rayed four-point star work, or should it be something else, like Polaris's own star mark or a small eye?
