Connection indicator — second set

Set one was four drawings of the same thing: a small mark in the footer's left cell. These are different in kind, not style — each answers a different question. Same real 260px footer strip, same tokens, both states.

F

Silent

Nothing at all while it works — the left cell is empty, the icons sit at the right. The footer only speaks when the socket is gone: the well tints, and one line of plain text appears. Absence as the "everything's fine" signal.

connected
not connected
no link · retry 2s

why the empty cell is honest

A healthy socket has nothing you can do about it, and a broken one announces itself: the composer refuses to send, the header stops streaming. The status line only has to confirm the thing you can't otherwise see — the retry cadence.

Watch: a completely empty left cell may read as "unfinished" on first load, before the socket has ever opened — if so, show the down line until it first connects. And the copy has to stay short: "retrying every 2s" truncates at 260px next to three footer icons, which is how this one ended up as "no link · retry 2s".

G

Readout

No mark: mono type with real numbers. Round-trip time to the box while it's up, and the retry counter while it isn't. Answers "is the potato healthy and how slow is it" — which a dot can never do.

reconnecting
link—retry 4

the three states it actually has

wait
link…
slow
link480ms

Needs a ping/pong frame on the existing socket (ws.ts, ~10 lines) and a retry counter in the reconnect timer. The number also earns its keep: it's the only way to see the potato itself getting slow vs. the internet getting slow.

Watch: a changing number in peripheral vision pulls the eye every 2s. Only re-measure on a 30s cadence (or on demand), and never animate the value.

H

Panel

A hardware front panel: WSS · SEARX, square lamps, mono labels. Tells you what is degraded, not just that something is — SearXNG already knows when it's in its hour-long engine cooldown, and today that fact is only visible in the transcript.

all good
wss searx
socket down
wss searx

the state that actually happens most

cool
wss searx

SearXNG's engine cooldown (search/searxng.go) is a real, already-tracked state that the UI currently hides — search silently degrades to the paid chain instead. Giving it a lamp turns a mystery into a readout.

Watch: two labeled lamps + three icons in 260px is the densest option here, and labels are 8.5px mono — legible on a phone, but only just. Three lamps (db?) would not fit, and SQLite can't fail independently of the process anyway.

I

Well

No mark at all — the strip's own surface carries it. Connected: a faint warm spill of light from the left edge of the carved well, as if the panel is lit from inside. Disconnected: the light leaves and the well slowly breathes a dim red wash.

connected
connected
reconnecting
reconnecting…

why this is the calmest one

Nothing in the footer is an object anymore — the state is a property of the room. It also can't be confused with the Pulsar/Daily unread dots, because there is no dot. The down state still has to be findable, so the breathing wash does the work the word alone can't.

Watch: the weakest at a glance — you cannot tell up from down from a still frame at arm's length; it relies on the word and on motion. It's also the only option that can be layered on top of any other (keep a mark and add the lit well), which might be its best use.

J

Heartbeat

A 1px trace along the strip: a blip each time the socket answers a ping. Alive = a real rhythm you can read at a glance, without a single word. Dead = a flatline with a break where the beat stopped.

connected
reconnecting

zoomed

up

Needs the same ping/pong frame as G, plus a rollover buffer of the last ~10 beats. Without real beats it's a decorative squiggle, which is the thing to avoid.

Watch: a 2s blip is a lot of motion for a footer that lives next to the settings gear; and it's the only option that looks like a graph, so it reads "status page", not "calm room".

Rejected from set 1 on the way here, for the record: a heuristic bar meter, a wifi ripple, a lighthouse sweep, a crescent "occultation" (reads as Do Not Disturb), and a browser-tab spinner variant (reads as loading, not disconnected). They were all still "a mark in the left cell" — the same failure mode as the dot.
