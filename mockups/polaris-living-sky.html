A living sky for the start screen

Everything combined: the deep-field parallax and Milky Way, twinkling stars, a comet every so often, and a pool of eight constellations drawn into four corner spots. Each spot picks one at random that is not already on screen. It connects star by star, holds, then disconnects in exactly the reverse order. Shown on a phone and on a desktop-width screen.

Move the pointer over a frame to shift the parallax.

Timing at normal speed. A constellation draws in 4 to 6 seconds, holds for 7, retracts the same way, then its spot rests for 4 to 12 seconds before choosing again, so usually two or three are visible. Shapes are randomly mirrored. The pool is Ursa Minor, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, Ursa Major, Orion, Leo, Scorpius and Cygnus. The comet now crosses roughly every 25 to 40 seconds.
